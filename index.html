Introduction

The rapid proliferation of online polling data has created a fragmented and often opaque information environment in which unrepresentative, methodologically weak, or even misleading “polls” circulate widely. This poses risks for public understanding and political analysis, as such data are frequently interpreted as credible measures of public opinion. Against this backdrop, there is a clear need for greater transparency regarding who produces polling data and under what methodological conditions.

Europe Elects: Truth in Polling is a publicly accessible database that systematically maps and profiles organisations across Europe that publish voting intention data. It focuses on actors that explicitly or implicitly claim to produce methodologically sound, empirically grounded, and statistically robust polling.

Overall, the initiative seeks to counter misinformation, promote accountability, and foster dialogue between polling organisations and data users. By offering a comprehensive and comparative overview of pollsters in Europe, it contributes to improving the reliability and interpretation of electoral data in both academic research and public discourse.

Not every listed organisation meets professional polling standards. The database documents variation in methodological practices and highlights cases where organisations diverge from commonly accepted industry norms. Without issuing categorical judgments, it enables users to critically assess the credibility of different data providers.

Polling has structural limits in some countries. In authoritarian regimes, even technically rigorous surveys may fail to capture genuine public opinion due to respondent self-censorship.
